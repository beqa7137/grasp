## Experiments (engine build)

### Four parts in a row

- **id:** `series`
- **Ask:** A page request needs 4 parts in turn: a load balancer, an app server, a database and a recommendations service. There is one copy of each, and each is 99.9% available: down 8.8 h a year, in outages of 1 h. How long is the request down a year?
- **Choice 1:** 8.8 h: a chain is as good as its weakest part
- **Choice 2:** 17.5 h: about twice that, as outages often coincide
- **Choice 3 (answer):** 35 h: every part’s outages count in full
- **Reveal:** 35 h: the request needs every part, so it fails whenever any one of them is down. Outages of 1 h that strike independently almost never coincide, so the downtime of 4 parts adds up: 4 × 8.76 h is about 35 h, and the availabilities multiply to 99.6%. A chain is always worse than its weakest part, and every hard dependency you add costs its whole downtime.
- **Hides while asking:** sample, request
- **Focus at the reveal:** timeline, ruler
- **While the answer plays:** Watch the request’s row: it collects every failure that reaches it.

### A second database

- **id:** `parallel`
- **Ask:** A load balancer, an app server and a database, one copy of each, and each down 8.8 h a year; recommendations are optional now. Give the database a second copy that fails independently of the first, and say traffic moves to it at once. How long is the database part down a year now?
- **Choice 1 (answer):** 32 s: only while both are down at once
- **Choice 2:** 53 min: a tenth, a copy buys one more nine
- **Choice 3:** 4.4 h: half, two copies share the failures
- **Reveal:** 32 s: the part is down only while both copies are down at once. Each is down 0.1% of the time, so both are down 0.1% of 0.1%: 99.9999% available, 32 s a year against 8.8 h. Unavailabilities multiply, so each independent copy adds 3 nines. The request is still down 17.5 h a year, because the two parts left with one copy now set it. And 32 s is the ceiling of the arithmetic, not a forecast: it holds only if the copies never fail together and traffic moves instantly.
- **Hides while asking:** after
- **Focus at the reveal:** timeline, ruler
- **While the answer plays:** Watch the database’s two rows and the strip under them.

### One more app server

- **id:** `weakest`
- **Ask:** The request passes 2 load balancers, 2 app servers and 1 database, every copy 99.9% available; recommendations are optional. It is down 8.8 h a year. Add a third app server. How long is the request down a year now?
- **Choice 1:** 4.4 h: half, as three app servers never fail together
- **Choice 2:** 5.9 h: a third less, as each server now carries a third of the traffic
- **Choice 3 (answer):** 8.8 h: unchanged, the app servers were never the limit
- **Reveal:** 8.8 h, unchanged: the request is down because of the database, a single copy down 8.8 h a year. The app servers were down together only 32 s a year as a pair, and 0.03 s as three. Polishing a strong part buys nothing you can measure: the weakest link caps the chain, so find the single point of failure before adding copies anywhere else.
- **Hides while asking:** sample, weakest, after
- **Focus at the reveal:** ruler, chain
- **While the answer plays:** Watch the app servers’ rows and the request’s row.

### Failures the copies share

- **id:** `shared`
- **Ask:** Two copies of every part the request needs: on paper it is down only 95 s a year. Now say 1 in 10 of a copy’s outages come from something its twin shares, such as the zone both run in or a bad deploy that reaches both. How long is the request down a year now?
- **Choice 1:** 104 s: a tenth more than on paper
- **Choice 2 (answer):** 2.6 h: about 100× what the paper says
- **Choice 3:** 26.3 h: as much as with one copy of each
- **Reveal:** 2.6 h, about 100× the paper’s 95 s. A second copy removes only the outages that are a copy’s own. A shared one takes both copies down together, so it passes straight through: 53 min a year for each part, however many copies it has. With 1 in 10 outages shared, copies buy at most one more nine, 99.99% a part and not 99.9999%. So spread copies across zones, roll changes out in stages and split users into cells: the fix is fewer shared causes, not more copies.
- **Hides while asking:** after
- **Focus at the reveal:** timeline, ruler
- **While the answer plays:** Watch the request’s row.

### Failover takes time

- **id:** `failover`
- **Ask:** Two copies of every part the request needs, failing independently: each part sends all its traffic to one copy and keeps the other as a standby. On paper the request fails only when both copies of a part are down at once, about once in 19 years. But moving traffic off a dead copy takes 5 min: health checks must notice, then the switch happens. About how many times a year does the request fail now?
- **Choice 1 (answer):** About 26 times: at every failure of a copy in use
- **Choice 2:** About 53 times: at every failure of either copy
- **Choice 3:** Still about once in 19 years: the standby was ready
- **Reveal:** About 26 times a year, 5 min each: 2.2 h a year instead of 95 s. Redundancy does not remove a copy’s failures, it shortens them: each of the 8.76 outages a year of the copy in use now ends when traffic has moved, after 5 min, not when the repair is done, after 1 h. Availability is MTBF / (MTBF + MTTR), and a standby turns the time to recover from the repair time into the failover time. Four nines a part is 53 min a year; with 8.76 failures that leaves about 6 min for each, so detection and the switch must be automatic, and rehearsed.
- **Hides while asking:** after
- **Focus at the reveal:** timeline, chain
- **While the answer plays:** Watch the request’s row.

### Three nines from seven copies

- **id:** `add-a-nine`
- **Ask:** This design has shared failures and a 5 min failover, as real ones do, and the request is down 20.6 h a year. Promise three nines: bring it under 8.76 h a year, 99.9%, with no more than the 7 copies it runs now.
- **Reveal:** Run 2 load balancers, 2 app servers and 2 databases, with recommendations optional: down 4.6 h a year, 99.947%. Moving one app server to the database removes the single point of failure, and making recommendations optional takes a whole part out of the chain; the third app server was buying nothing. The 4.6 h left are shared outages and failovers, which no further copy removes: the next nine comes from fewer shared causes and a faster switch. That leaves 4.1 h of the 8.76 h error budget for a bad year and risky deploys: promise below what you expect.
- **Hides while asking:** nothing
- **Focus at the reveal:** ruler, chain
