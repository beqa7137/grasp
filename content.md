## Experiments (engine build)

### Ten times the traffic

- **id:** `tenfold`
- **Ask:** Today 1,000 requests a second arrive, and every tier has room to spare. Each request passes a load balancer and an app server, then makes 2 queries to the database. Traffic grows 10×, to 10,000/s. Which tier runs out first?
- **Choice 1:** The load balancers: 2 of 5,000/s each
- **Choice 2:** The app servers: 4 of 1,500/s each
- **Choice 3 (answer):** The database: one primary of 8,000 queries/s
- **Reveal:** The database, at 4,000/s: each request costs it 2 queries, so its 8,000 queries a second carry only 4,000 requests a second, fewer than the app servers' 6,000/s or the load balancers' 10,000/s. Compare tiers by their limit in requests a second, never by their raw size. At 10,000/s only 4,000/s are served: the app servers, full at 6,000/s, shed 4,000/s, and the database sheds 2,000/s more. Cache the reads or add 2 or more replicas first; the app servers come next, and the load balancers end exactly 100% busy.
- **Hides while asking:** db
- **Focus at the reveal:** primary, sink

### Cache the reads

- **id:** `cache`
- **Ask:** The database tops out at 4,000/s. Each request makes 1.8 reads and 0.2 writes, 2 queries in all, against its 8,000 queries a second. Put a cache in front of the reads, and 90% of them hit it. How many requests a second can the database take now?
- **Choice 1:** About 8,000/s: twice as many, the cache takes half the work
- **Choice 2 (answer):** About 21,000/s: 5× as many, the writes still reach it
- **Choice 3:** 40,000/s: 10× as many, like the hit rate
- **Reveal:** 21,053/s, 5.3× as many: the cache answers 1.62 of each request's 1.8 reads, so only 0.18 reads and the 0.2 writes reach the database, 0.38 queries a request instead of 2. Its 8,000 queries a second now serve 21,053/s, not the 40,000/s a 90% hit rate suggests: writes always reach the database. All 10,000/s are served now, and the app servers, 83.3% busy, are the busiest tier.
- **Hides while asking:** after
- **Focus at the reveal:** primary

### Two more replicas

- **id:** `replay`
- **Ask:** At 20,000/s, the database takes 4,000 writes a second and, after the cache, 3,600 reads. The 2 read replicas share the reads, each 72.5% busy. Add 2 more. How busy is each replica now?
- **Choice 1:** About 36%: half as busy, the work splits 4 ways
- **Choice 2 (answer):** About 61%: a little less, part of the work does not split
- **Choice 3:** About 11%: a replica only serves reads, now split 4 ways
- **Reveal:** 61.3%: every replica replays every write, 4,000 a second, which takes 50% of its 8,000 queries a second however many replicas share the reads. Only the 3,600 reads a second split: 1,800 each with 2 replicas, 900 with 4. Twice the replicas raise their limit from 27,586/s to 32,653/s, 18.4% more, and no number of them passes 40,000/s, where replaying the writes alone fills a replica. Replicas scale reads, not writes.
- **Hides while asking:** after, replay
- **Focus at the reveal:** replicas

### Lose a zone

- **id:** `zone`
- **Ask:** The load balancers and app servers are spread evenly over zones A, B and C, and no tier runs above 74.1%; all 10,000/s are served. Zone A fails with every instance in it, and a replica takes over from the primary. How many requests a second are shed?
- **Choice 1:** None: every tier has room, at least 25.9% to spare
- **Choice 2:** About 3,300/s: a third, the zone's share
- **Choice 3 (answer):** 1,000/s: a tenth, more than the busiest tier left can take
- **Reveal:** 1,000/s, a tenth: the 6 app servers left can take 9,000/s, so the 74.1% they ran at becomes 111.1% of what they can serve. Losing one zone of three multiplies the load on what is left by 1.5× when a tier is spread evenly, so size every tier so that what survives the largest zone still carries the peak. The load balancers end exactly 100% busy, with nothing to spare. The database loses its primary and a replica yet keeps room: a replica became the primary (writes fail until that finishes, seconds to minutes), and the one left runs at 42.8%.
- **Hides while asking:** after, zones
- **Focus at the reveal:** app, lb

### A slow database

- **id:** `slow`
- **Ask:** Each app server has 100 workers, and a request holds one for its whole 50 ms: at 6,000/s, 50 of each server's 100 are busy. A network fault between the app servers and the database adds 450 ms to every request, so each takes 500 ms, though the database does no more work. How many requests a second are served?
- **Choice 1 (answer):** 1,200/s: a fifth, the workers cap it
- **Choice 2:** 6,000/s: all of them, each one slower
- **Choice 3:** 3,000/s: half, as the 50 idle workers fill up
- **Reveal:** 1,200/s, a fifth: requests in flight equal throughput times latency (Little's law). Each server's 100 workers hold at most 100 requests at once, and at 500 ms each that is 200/s; 6 servers serve 1,200/s. The other 4,800/s are shed at the app servers while their CPUs sit 13.3% busy: waiting on the database used up the workers in front of it. An autoscaler watching CPU sees 13.3% and adds nothing, or even scales in: scale and load-test on pool saturation and latency. Serving 6,000/s at 500 ms needs 3,000 requests in flight. Set timeouts, fix the slow network path, and shed load early rather than only growing the pool.
- **Hides while asking:** after
- **Focus at the reveal:** app

### Challenge: 16,000/s with a zone to spare

- **id:** `challenge`
- **Ask:** Serve 16,000/s with every tier keeping at least 30% headroom, nothing shed when zone A fails, and at most 29 machines in all; a cache keeps a full copy in each of the 3 zones.
- **Reveal:** The fleet that fits: 6 load balancers, 17 app servers, a primary of 8,000 queries/s, 2 replicas and the cache's 3 nodes, 29 machines; the busiest tier runs at 62.7%. Zone A holds 2 of the load balancers and 6 of the app servers, so losing it leaves 4 load balancers 80% busy and 11 app servers 97% busy, while one replica takes over as primary and the other carries every read at 76%. With 16 app servers zone A would hold 6, and the 10 left would shed 1,000/s; with no replica the primary would fall with its zone and every write with it.
- **Focus at the reveal:** lb, app, db
